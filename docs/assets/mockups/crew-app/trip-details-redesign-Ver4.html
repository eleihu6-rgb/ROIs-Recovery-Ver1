Crew App · Trip Details — timeline rail (Ver4)

The duty reads top-to-bottom as a clock, not as a stack of cards. Each sector is two nodes — departure and arrival — joined by the rail; the gap to the next departure is the turn time. Scheduled time sits in the gutter; estimated and actual hang off it. Duty totals form one flat band.

Theme
1 · Shared duty title

The hero names all six flights and the routing once; the rail never repeats a trip title.

2 · Times stay paired

STD with ETD/ATD on the departure node; STA with ETA/ATA on the arrival node. A pair never splits across columns.

3 · Duty totals once

FDP · DP · Block · Rest are one flat band under the hero, independent of sector count.

4 · Calendar in the header

The iPhone Calendar action becomes a nav-bar icon — the 50 pt strip is reclaimed for the schedule.

1 · iPhone — compact (420 × 912)

Same six-sector duty. The rail keeps every scheduled time in one aligned gutter, so the day scans as a sequence of departures and arrivals; turn times make the gaps explicit.

2 · iPhone Duo inner screen — wide (951 × 669)

All six sectors fit without scrolling: six two-node segments plus five turn gaps. The rail is the only vertical structure — there are no per-sector cards to stack.

2
Scheduled vs actual, read across

The gutter is the plan; the inline ETD/ATD is the reality. A delay shows as a second value on the same line, not another row.

3
Turns are visible

turn 35 min between sectors answers the question a table cannot: how much ground time is left.

5
Ops stay with the departure

Boarding · door close · off-block and the gate/stand path sit under the departure node they belong to; belt stays on arrival.

6
Scales to six

Six sectors add six short segments (~56 pt each), not six 138 pt cards.
